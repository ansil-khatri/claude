# 05 · Content plan: guides, cost pillars, tools

## 1. How every article is written (non-negotiable)

The goal is to **help a business owner fix their problem**, even if they never hire us. That's what earns rankings, AI citations and trust.

1. **Start from the owner's situation, in their words.** First line is a real-sounding moment: "It's 7:40 on a Friday and three people have DM'd asking if you're open Sunday." Then name the problem, then the answer.
2. **Answer first.** The direct answer (a number, a yes/no, a recommendation) appears in the first 2–3 sentences. Google's AI Overviews and ChatGPT lift exactly this.
3. **Show real numbers.** Price ranges with what drives them, fees worked out on a real example ("$40k/month in card sales: PayPal $1,641 vs Stripe $1,310"), timelines in weeks.
4. **Be honest, including "you don't need us".** "If you have under 50 orders a week, a Square Online page is enough. Here's how to set it up." This is the strongest trust signal there is, and it's what Google's helpful-content system rewards.
5. **Plain English.** No "leverage", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "in today's digital landscape". Explain any tech word the first time ("Flutter, which builds the iPhone and Android app from one codebase").
6. **One visual per section** in the site style (real-looking screenshots, tables, before/after), not stock photos.
7. **Written by a real person, reviewed by a real person.** Author box with name, photo, role, LinkedIn. AI drafts are fine as a start; a human who has done the work must add the specifics, the opinions and the examples. Pure AI text is what the owner calls "spam" and Google agrees.
8. **Length = what it takes.** Cost pillars 2,500–3,500 words; problem posts 1,200–1,800; comparisons 1,800–2,500.
9. **Soft CTA, once in the middle, once at the end.** "Want us to look at yours? Book a free 30-minute call." Plus the contextual link to the money page.
10. **Update dates for real.** Cost guides are refreshed every quarter, with `dateModified` changed only when content actually changes.

## 4. Tools (calculators): build 3 at launch, 2 later

Tools rank for their own keywords, get linked by other sites and convert well because the result screen shows the owner their own loss.

| Tool | Ranks for | Result screen shows | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **App cost calculator** (P1) | app cost calculator, how much will my app cost | Range from our real tiers + what drives it + weeks | "Get a fixed quote in 30 minutes" |
| **Delivery commission calculator** (P1) | doordash commission calculator, how much do delivery apps take | "You paid $X to delivery apps last year; with your own ordering at 3% you'd keep $Y" | Restaurants / online-ordering |
| **Payment fee calculator** (P1) | stripe vs square fees calculator, credit card processing fees calculator | Annual fees per provider on their volume (the payments page already has this logic) | Payments |
| Missed-call calculator (P2) | missed calls cost calculator | "~$X/month in jobs you didn't answer" | AI receptionist |
| No-show cost calculator (P2) | salon no show cost | Lost revenue + what deposits recover | Salons / booking-app |

Each tool: server-rendered intro (300–500 words explaining the math and assumptions), the calculator (client component), the result with links, FAQ, `SoftwareApplication`/`WebApplication` schema.

## 6. Other channels that feed the same content

- **Google Business Profile posts:** not available (see `02`).
- **YouTube:** a 3–6 minute video per cost pillar and per rescue guide (screen + face). Embed in the article (VideoObject schema). YouTube is the #2 search engine and is heavily cited by AI answers.
- **Reddit & Quora (honestly):** team members answer questions in r/smallbusiness, r/shopify, r/lovable, r/restaurantowners **as themselves**, with real help, linking only when the guide genuinely answers it. No fake accounts, no astroturfing (it gets accounts banned and brands named and shamed). Reddit threads are among the most-cited sources in AI answers, so being a helpful named voice there matters.
- **LinkedIn:** founder posts with the same stories, aimed at agency owners (white-label pipeline).
